Harder tests found fixes beyond the model

24 hand-written synthetic calls were evaluated before and after targeted fixes. Eight additional cases were then written and measured separately. Every person, business and contact detail in the fixtures is invented.

What changed

  • Saved identities match canonically equivalent Unicode accents, smart apostrophes and hyphens. Originals retain their exact source text.
  • Phone extensions spelled out or preceded by # are included in redaction. Explicit phone cues allow additional digits-only and international formats without treating ordinary invoice IDs or dates as phones.
  • Apostrophe email local parts are captured completely.
  • VTT voice annotations raise a speaker question when statistical detection misses the name.
  • Explicit “we work for” and “I work for” company context can raise a question for an otherwise allowed public-tool name. Ordinary tool usage stays visible.
  • Longer call-specific corrections run before shorter answers, so a full-address correction can override a partial street-name answer. A selected number can be hidden before a capitalized word.

The name model was unchanged.

Four main-set calls still need fuller manual review

  • Surname alone: Velloren was not linked to the saved Nika Velloren record or flagged as unknown.
  • Street address: the street name raised a question, but answering that question left the house number and suite.
  • Client website: an unhelpful question about “https” left the identifying domain visible.
  • Indirect identity: a unique business description remained visible.

Manual phrase corrections removed these planted details in the test. Address and indirect-identity detection remain outside the app's automatic names, companies, emails and phones scope.

A clean status means the app's questions are resolved. It does not prove that all identifying information has been removed. Review the original and clean copy before approving an export.

How the measurement works

  1. Import each case into a fresh isolated vault, with only its declared saved records and the invented allowed owner.
  2. Capture initial status, actual review questions and labeled misses. Held calls are reported as held, never scored as safe.
  3. Answer only the questions the app actually asks, using the fixture's expected choices through the normal identity operations. Re-score independently, including details the detector never asked about.
  4. Simulate exhaustive review by selecting remaining labeled source phrases through the app's correction operation. Verify the approved exported file, neutral filename, original preservation and lack of premature exports.

The final stage uses an answer key. Its 32/32 result validates the correction and export workflow on these fixtures. It does not mean students will notice every missed detail, or that the detector has 100% accuracy.

No specified business phrases or the allowed owner were lost in the 32 final reviewed copies. These are exact phrase checks, not a semantic quality assessment. The eight follow-up cases are a small holdout, not an estimate of real-world accuracy.
